# N176 interview-stage research — new owner requirement

Status: intake proposal; implementation, instruments and independent acceptance pending. This is a new product requirement, not a reopened third review of the previously checked source. Existing failures, review counts and the expired manual-run budget remain intact.

The owner requests that Interview Preparation research and cite the likely interview stages and the five most likely questions for each stage.

## Observable additions

- N176-26: For the selected company, role and available location/seniority context, an explicit research action produces an ordered likely-stage map. Prefer employer/recruiter instructions and official hiring sources. Show supporting source links, relevant excerpts, publication date when available and retrieval time. Distinguish documented stages, dated candidate reports and inferred stages; unknowns and conflicting evidence remain visible. Do not fabricate stages, citations or probability percentages.
- N176-27: Every proposed stage includes exactly five ranked, distinct likely questions tailored to that stage and role. Explain why each question is likely. Cite source evidence where it supports the prediction; otherwise clearly identify the question as an inference. Do not represent predicted questions as confirmed employer questions. Sparse evidence must remain explicit rather than silently substituting a generic list represented as research.
- N176-28: Stage/question research persists with the application's preparation workspace. Users can edit it and select stage-specific questions for the existing rehearsal, answer review and repair tools. Preserve edits and source provenance on refresh. Company/role/context changes make the affected research visibly stale. Any generated personal answers continue to use the exact resume/cover pair, shared tailoring strength and adversarial-review pipeline.
- N176-29: Expose research progress, source coverage, uncertainty, no-results, unavailable-provider, request failure, cancellation and retry states. Retain previous results and edits on failure; fence late results from a different application or research context. Live verification must exercise the stage map, five questions per stage, citation links, sparse/failure recovery, save/reopen and transfer into rehearsal on the deployed candidate.

## Advantage and constraints

The advantage over a standalone chat is a persistent, cited interview-stage map linked to the tracked application and directly usable as stage-specific rehearsal questions alongside the same grounded answer materials. A generic checklist or an uncited model prediction does not satisfy the requirement.

Community interview reports are anecdotal and may be outdated or refer to a different role/location. Label their limits and do not promise the actual process or a guaranteed question. Prefer a clear insufficient-evidence result to invented attribution.

Discover and independently check suitable existing research instruments before choosing implementation paths. The existing positive-news company brief is not evidence of a hiring process. New public-source retrieval triggers security/privacy, reliability/cost, citation-quality and accessibility review. Treat retrieved pages and pasted instructions as untrusted data. No new provider, paid developer dispatch, login, installation, private-data disclosure or deployment command is authorized by this intake artifact.

No product write set or gate proof is invented here. The checked architecture, exact callers, scope and test oracles must be amended before implementation. Previously accepted source remains historical for its original requirements; it does not establish these four new criteria.
