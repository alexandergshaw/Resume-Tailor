### R-283 | area: drive-modal-mount | parallel-safe: yes | automatable: partly

**Summary:** The final Drive wave: the save and download controls are mounted in the post-tailor files modal, so **the feature is complete end to end** — a user can save the résumé and the cover letter to Google Drive as native Google Docs, and download them back. Unlike R-279/R-280/R-281 nothing here is inert, and unlike R-282 the boundary it declined to cross is now crossed. Six things are worth keeping from this wave, in descending order of what it would cost to lose them: a join pinned by nothing, where deleting the single prop the whole wave exists to add left the entire shipped suite green; three of the design's own criteria that were covered on one path out of eleven, re-derived away before they were written, or never built at all; the byte-level proof that unblurred typing reaches the uploaded .docx, plus the nuance about which link in that chain is actually load-bearing; a jsdom trap whose reach was measured rather than assumed; the residuals this feature knowingly ships with; and the fact that `DocumentPreviewDialog.js` now sits seven lines under a hard cap.

A failure at step 10 with steps 1-8 green means the cookie survived nothing and the fault is in deployment or in a `@supabase/ssr` upgrade, not in this wave's code. A failure at step 12 or 13 with every gate green is the expected shape of this feature's next serious defect, which is why they are here: the two paths that can destroy a user's document are the two the automated suite reaches least.

**THE JOIN THAT WAS PINNED BY NOTHING — this is the centrepiece.** Removing `drive={drive}` from `DocumentPreviewMount` — the one prop this entire wave exists to add — left **the whole shipped suite green.** `DocumentPreviewDialog`'s `drive = DEFAULT_DRIVE` default silently substituted a `status: "unconfigured"` object, `DriveActions` rendered nothing at all, and every live-region assertion still passed because the default carried a valid `announcement`. The seam was pinned in two halves that never met: the dialog was tested against a **mocked** `saveToDrive`, the hook was tested down to the bytes, and no test asserted the two were connected to each other. A user would have opened the files modal and found no Drive controls, with 7408 tests agreeing that everything was fine.

Fixed two ways, and **the deletion matters more than the test.** The permanent join test mounts the real tree and unzips the captured request, so the wire is now held by behaviour. But `DEFAULT_DRIVE` is **deleted entirely** and `drive` is a required prop that throws loudly when absent — because a silent fallback is precisely what let a missing wire hide behind a four-hundred-file green suite, and no future test can be relied on to be written. The deletion also unified a second inconsistency it was quietly masking: `handleSaveToDrive` read `drive?.saveToDrive?.()` while the JSX read `drive.leadingLine` unguarded, so `drive={null}` crashed and `drive={undefined}` rendered a Drive-less modal — two different behaviours for the same misconfiguration, now one. **The general form: a default value for a prop that represents a wire converts a missing connection into a working-looking product, and defaults are invisible to every test that does not deliberately omit the prop.**

**THE SEAM ITSELF IS CORRECT, VERIFIED AT THE BYTES — and the obvious reading of it is inverted.** An independent reviewer mounted the real component tree, stubbed only `fetch`, typed two paragraphs into the contenteditable, clicked Save **without ever blurring**, pulled the file part out of the captured `FormData`, unzipped it and read `word/document.xml`: the freshly typed text is there and the stored pre-edit text is gone. The chain that carries it is `editorRef.innerText → activePayload() → saveToDrive → performSave → attemptOneScope → buildPreviewBlob → previewBlobArgs → resolveDocumentBlob → POST body`.

**Record the nuance, because it reverses what the code's own comment implies.** Dropping `commitDraft()` from the wrapper leaves the **BYTES correct** — `activePayload()` reads the live DOM ref, not parent state. What breaks is the **staleness hash**: it recomputes from stored state while the hash that was SENT came from the draft, the two disagree, and the modal then renders *simultaneously* `Saved 1 document to Drive.`, `The Drive copy differs from the document shown here.`, and a `Download older Drive copy` button — a success message, its own contradiction, and an offer to download the older copy of a file it just uploaded. **`commitDraft()` is load-bearing for the BADGE, not for the content.** Anyone tidying the wrapper on the theory that the commit keeps the bytes in sync will delete it, keep correct bytes, and ship a modal that argues with itself.

**AC-E14 WAS PINNED ON 1 OF 11 PATHS.** The guarantee is that no Drive action ever writes the modal's local `busy`/`notice`/`error` maps — a Drive write into `busy` would freeze the modal. Mutating `busyActive` red exactly one test; mutating **`anyBusy` was GREEN**, and `anyBusy` alone gates **7 of the 10** controls; mutating the `error` map was GREEN too. So the single most damaging version of the bug was the uncovered one. It is now held by a props spy plus a behavioural check across all ten controls — focus picker, framing select, scan posting, combine format and combine download via `anyBusy`; version control, highlight toggle, revise textarea, revise button and download .docx via `busyActive` — through a full `disconnected → consentPending → saving → connected` cycle, with a positive control in which a deliberate write disables all ten. **Enumerate the ten in the TEST, never in a criterion.** Four readers produced four different counts from the prose, which is exactly why the rule is the flat one: *never write the map.*

**AC-S6a'S BYTE COMPARISON NEVER EXISTED.** It was re-derived in Wave 1 into four narrower criteria, the `[node]` one was written, and the byte comparison itself quietly was not — a criterion can be decomposed out of existence and leave every one of its children green. It now covers rows 1-3 (verbatim-serve) by comparing the real `buildPreviewBlob` against the real `downloadDocxFiles`. **Two things a future maintainer must know before touching it.** It requires `vi.useFakeTimers({ toFake: ["Date"] })` and nothing wider — faking **all** timers hangs JSZip's internal scheduling — and without a frozen clock it is genuinely flaky, because JSZip stamps wall-clock time into the archive: back-to-back builds come out identical, builds ~3s apart differ, both measured. And **row 4 (the hand-edited restored chip) is excluded deliberately**: on that row the Drive path is CORRECT and the local download is defective, so pinning them together would oblige Drive to reproduce a bug. An excluded row with a written reason is a finding; an excluded row without one is a hole.

**AC-A4 WAS NEVER BUILT, AND THIS WAVE IS WHAT MADE IT REACHABLE.** Focus restoration after the consent window closes did not exist anywhere, so a keyboard user who connected Drive from the modal landed on `<body>` — harmless while nothing mounted the controls, user-facing the moment they did. Now implemented: focus captured at click time, a `useLayoutEffect` that snapshots on entering `consentPending` and restores on leaving, only if focus is unchanged since, falling back to the save control. **Record the two traps its test had to handle**, because both produce a test that passes against code that does nothing: **jsdom does not move focus on a synthetic `MouseEvent`**, so the test must focus first and then click — otherwise it passes against an implementation that never calls `focus()` at all; and a **`useLayoutEffect` cleanup runs BEFORE React detaches a node**, while a passive cleanup runs after the browser has already reset focus, so the passive version restores focus onto a node that is on its way out.

**A jsdom TRAP WORTH RECORDING, WITH ITS REACH MEASURED.** jsdom 29.1.1 implements **no `HTMLElement.prototype.innerText` at all** — `el.innerText` is `undefined`, verified directly (`'innerText' in el === false`). Both `commitDraft` and `activePayload` read it. **Reach is ZERO**: there are exactly two readers, both behind `mode === "edit"`, and the only other test file that renders the dialog never enters edit mode — so no existing test was silently asserting against `undefined`. Both halves belong in the record: the trap is real and any new test touching the editor needs the polyfill, **and it was checked rather than assumed.** "Nothing else is affected" is a claim with a measurement behind it here, not a hope.

**THE CAP IS SEVEN LINES AWAY.** `DocumentPreviewDialog.js` is **993 of a hard 1000**. The next change that touches it must **extract into `lib/`** — not trim comments, not delete the explanatory blocks this entry points at, and not relocate lines into another already-capped file. Buying headroom by deleting the comments that record why `DEFAULT_DRIVE` is gone or why `toFake: ["Date"]` is narrow is the worst of the available moves, because it spends the wave's findings to buy space for a change that has not been designed yet.

**Known flake, recorded so the next person does not re-diagnose it:** `app/hooks/useDriveDocuments.connect.test.js` — *"Overwrite retries with onConflict exactly 'overwrite'"* failed in three multi-file runs whose mutations could not possibly reach it, and passed 4/4 in isolation. A promise deliberately not wrapped in `act()`. This is the sibling of R-282's *"Save as a new Doc retries with onConflict exactly 'new'"* flake in the same file, from the same cause.

**Still open from earlier waves and explicitly NOT fixed here** — none of these is a regression of this wave, and none is an oversight: the create-after-persistence-failure residual (R-281 — create succeeds, persistence fails, the user returns in a fresh context, and there is no id anywhere to key on); **PKCE on both OAuth providers** — an attacker who can read an unconsumed state can bind a victim's account to their own Drive; `DriveButton`'s failure string with no `aria-live` region; ~145 lines of pure, node-testable logic still inside a `"use client"` file; a download filename that disagrees with the Doc's actual name in Drive on **every** post-reload download of a renamed document, not merely on some (R-282's own note is sharpened there with the reason); and two buttons named `Close` on mobile (pre-existing, not introduced by the Drive controls).

**Amended (manual regression pass) — steps 12 and 13 are new, and they were added for the same reason the rest of R-277…R-283 needed amending.** An adversarial pass over the seven Drive cases found the automated claims sound at the exact lines cited and the save/download wiring continuous end to end, but it also found that the two paths capable of destroying a user's document were reachable by no step in this document: the `files.update` PATCH branch was flown only at the transport level, and the conflict prompt — where R-281's data-loss BLOCKER lived through two correct fixes — had no manual step for any of its three options. Both gaps are structural rather than accidental: no test can edit a Doc in Drive between two saves, and no stubbed transport can observe what Google did with a request. **Recorded here rather than filed as a residual**, because unlike PKCE or the `aria-live` region these needed no code to close — only someone running the steps. Step 11 remains the acceptance check; 12 and 13 are the two things it does not touch.
