## B. Findings

### F1 (MAJOR) — `WEAK_OPENERS` cannot be imported; the "adopt by import, canary-pinned" reuse decision rests on a non-existent export
§3.1, §11 and ledger **S-6** present `WEAK_OPENERS` as "**ADOPTED by import** (one source of truth)", "**reused as-is**" (§11's definition of *adopted*), with "a canary test [that] pins the shared list". First-hand: `WEAK_OPENERS` is a module-internal `const` at critique.js:9 — only `critiqueBullet`/`critiqueResume`/`renderCritique` are exported, and no re-export exists anywhere in `hello-world`.
- The import option is **not available** without editing `critique.js` to add `export`. Adding an export purely so another module can reach an internal symbol is the exact anti-pattern the loop warns against (brief standing rule 6 / subagent-edit hazards), and it drags `critique.js`'s module (→ `parseEmployment.js`) into the pure-core load graph.
- This is **internally inconsistent** with the design's own treatment of `containsTerm` (§11), which correctly rejected importing an equally module-internal symbol and defaulted to reimplement/extract. The design applied the right reasoning to `containsTerm` but not to the symmetric `WEAK_OPENERS`.
- **Why it matters:** the plan inherits S-6 as a live, canary-pinned import decision. The TDD/impl seat will either (a) try to import and fail, (b) add an export to a `lib/resume/` file (anti-pattern, and outside N106's clean additive-only blast radius claim in §11), or (c) silently diverge to a fork with no ledger authority.
- **Fix direction:** treat `WEAK_OPENERS` exactly like `containsTerm` — default to **fork/reimplement** (a 12-item lexicon, trivial) inside `mechanicalDetectors.js`, OR extract to a neutral `lib/text/` util in a SEPARATE chunk. Drop the "adopt by import / canary-pinned shared list" framing from §3.1, §11 and S-6; correct §11's "adopted = reused as-is" for this row. The same-polarity analysis stays valid; only the *mechanism* (import) is wrong.

### F2 (MAJOR) — the design silently resolves a self-contradiction inside the FINAL AC on whether skeleton categories enter `coverage.evaluatedCategories` on embedded
The AC r2 is internally inconsistent on this exact point:
- **AC §0 line 50**, **AC-3 I2 (line 132)**, and **I6 (line 237)**: the embedded deterministic skeleton of `consistency`/`unsupported-authority` does NOT place those categories in `evaluatedCategories`; only the 4 floor categories appear on embedded.
- **AC-8 (line 170)**: "`unsupported-authority` **appears in** `evaluatedCategories` on embedded only for its deterministic skeleton."

The design's §3.2 and §5 adopt the **ABSENT** reading (embedded ⇒ exactly the 4 floor categories; skeleton may emit a flag without the category entering `evaluatedCategories`). That is the **safe** reading (underclaim; `complete` stays false) and matches the load-bearing §0/AC-3/I6 clauses — so the *structure* is sound. But the design does **not flag the conflict**.
- **Why it matters:** the 4b/TDD seat binds to the AC verbatim. An author reading AC-8 line 170 could legitimately write: *embedded path, incoherent-authority draft ⇒ assert `unsupported-authority ∈ coverage.evaluatedCategories`.* The design's §5 makes that test permanently RED (it emits only the 4 floor), and the implementer cannot satisfy both AC-8-line-170 and AC-3-I2/§5. A FINAL/TERMINATING AC cannot absorb this in another round, so the design is the correct place to surface it.
- **Fix direction:** add an explicit note in §3.2/§5 that the design resolves AC-8's line-170 parenthetical ("appears in evaluatedCategories … for its deterministic skeleton") AGAINST §0/AC-3-I2/I6 by **excluding** skeleton-only categories from `evaluatedCategories`, and flag the AC contradiction to the orchestrator (an AC erratum / propagation note), so the TDD seat encodes the §0/I6 reading and does not write the opposing AC-8 assertion.

### F3 (MINOR) — injected-judge FAILURE semantics are unspecified (the brief's "flaky judge" question)
§4 step 3 does `await options.judge(judgeRequest)`; §5's `computeCoverage({hasJudge, judgeEvaluatedCategories})` assumes a well-formed judge result. The design specifies what happens to a judge flag with a **bad span id** (dropped, §4 step 4) and to a judge **claiming a floor category** in coverage (restricted, step 5), but is silent on a PRESENT judge that **rejects/throws, times out, or returns malformed output** (e.g. `undefined`, `evaluatedCategories` missing/not an array). The brief explicitly asks whether a flaky/absent judge degrades to mechanical-only cleanly; the design answers only the *absent* case.
- Direction note: this is the SAFE direction regardless (a throw yields no result, not an overclaim), so it is not a blocker — but the plan will have to invent it, and a malformed-but-non-throwing judge result feeding `computeCoverage` is a latent overclaim path if `judgeEvaluatedCategories` is coerced loosely.
- **Fix direction:** pin, in §4/§5, that (a) a judge rejection either propagates or degrades to `engineMode:"full"` mechanical-only (decide one), and (b) a malformed/missing `judgeEvaluatedCategories` is treated as **"no LLM categories reported"** (∩ with `LLM_CATEGORIES` yields ∅ ⇒ `complete:false`), never as full coverage — so the honesty coupling cannot be broken by a sloppy judge.

### F4 (MINOR) — judge-supplied FLAGS are not restricted to `LLM_CATEGORIES` (asymmetry with the coverage restriction)
§4 step 5 restricts `judgeResult.evaluatedCategories` to `LLM_CATEGORIES ∩ reported`, but steps 4/6 route judge **flags** through containment + dedupe without restricting their `category`. A judge could emit a `missing-keyword`/`repetition`/etc. flag the mechanical floor did not produce; it passes `assertContainedFlag` and AC-15 (valid enum, valid span) and is merged. No AC is violated (no overclaim), but the asymmetry is unstated: coverage says "the judge cannot claim a floor category," while flags let it inject one.
- **Fix direction:** either state explicitly that judge flags in any 7-enum category are intentionally allowed (deduped against mechanical flags), or restrict judge flags to `LLM_CATEGORIES` symmetrically with the evaluatedCategories restriction. Pick one so the plan/TDD has a determinate rule.

### F5 (MINOR) — AC-16 single-analyzer census vs the six exported `detect*` functions + `selectAuthorityReference`
§1's module map lists `mechanicalDetectors.js` exporting six `detect*` functions (each a document-weakness detector) and `referenceSelect.js` exporting `selectAuthorityReference`. AC-16 requires "exactly ONE document-weakness analyzer" and a census "canaried against the known `reviewDocuments` export." §10 asserts "a validator/enum export does not trip it" but does not address the **six exported weakness detectors**, which are plausibly "document-weakness analyzers" by the census's own description.
- Risk: the census is either **zero-power** (matches only the `reviewDocuments` token — then it cannot catch a genuine second analyzer, the trap `loop-traps-search` names) or **over-sensitive** (reds on the six `detect*` exports).
- **Fix direction:** have the design state the census **discriminator** — the public analyzer is the one returning the `ReviewResult` shape `{flags, unresolvedQualifications, coverage}`; `detect*` (spans→flags) and `selectAuthorityReference` (draft→{mode,referenceSpans}) have different signatures and do not count. OR make the detectors module-internal (tests import the file directly; consumers import only `reviewDocuments`), which also tightens AC-16's public-surface claim.
