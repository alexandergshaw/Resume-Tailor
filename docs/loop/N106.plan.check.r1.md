## Findings

### F1 (MAJOR) — the #1 overclaim sub-path (2b: resolved-but-malformed judge) is not commissioned as a 4b test, and the AC-12 seam-map enumeration omits it
Location: §4 (4b hand-off table, AC-12 row + the "mutant-only" subsection); design §5 F3.

The three overclaim sub-paths were attacked:
- **2a** (computeCoverage sets `complete:true` independently): GUARDED — mutant (a) `isSuperset→true` reds AC-3 floor-clean negative + AC-15 coupling; both are commissioned 4b rows.
- **2c** (swallow-and-claim: judge throws ⇒ must be mechanical-only): GUARDED — AC-12 seam-map row pins `throw⇒mechanical`, and AC-15 coupling (`mechanical-only ⟹ complete:false`) swept over every fixture; mutant (b) reds.
- **2b** (judge RESOLVES but returns malformed/missing `evaluatedCategories` ⇒ ∅ ⇒ `complete:false`, `engineMode:"full"`): **NOT guarded by a commissioned 4b test.** This is a FOURTH, distinct coverage outcome (design §5: `engineMode:"full"` yet `complete:false`), separate from throw (which gives `engineMode:"mechanical-only"`). The AC-12 seam-map row enumerates exactly three outcomes — `all-3⇒full/true`, `none⇒mechanical/false`, `throw⇒mechanical` — and OMITS the resolved-malformed case. The only place 2b appears is the §4 mutant-rationale line (c) ("coerce missing evaluatedCategories to 'all LLM' … reds malformed-judge fixture") and the risk-table step-1 row. A mutation kill presupposes the fixture already exists; nothing in the per-AC 4b table commissions it, so a TDD seat encoding from the table can ship without it — and then mutant (c) has no red (survives or must be invented at step 9).

Why it matters: 2b is the exact coercion the design calls the "latent overclaim path" (§4 step-3 note). It is the #1 safety direction (`complete:true`/legible-full over an unanalyzed category). Leaving it to a mutant-rationale mention, while the seam-map table enumerates its three siblings, is the single most important revision.

Fix direction: add an explicit 4b seam-map sub-case (AC-12 or AC-3 row): inject a fixed fake judge that RESOLVES with `evaluatedCategories` missing / non-array / garbage, and assert `coverage.engineMode==="full"` AND `coverage.complete===false` AND `reportedLlmCategories`/`evaluatedCategories` excludes any LLM category. This makes the seam-map enumeration four-way and gives mutant (c) a committed red.

### F2 (MAJOR) — judge-side span containment is not commissioned as a 4b test; AC-1 universal post-condition is zero-power against a judge-merge bypass
Location: §2 Step 4 green ("AC-1 containment post-condition over every fixture + union/synth drop"); §4 AC-1 row (Stub S); §4 mutant list ("emit-chokepoint-bypass mutant (detector pushes directly)"); design §6/S-8.

The plan guards MECHANICAL-flag containment well: Stub S (synth `"gen-0"` / union `"s1+s2"`) reds AC-1(a)/(b); the universal post-condition runs over every fixture; the emit-bypass mutant is scoped to "detector pushes directly." But the attack is judge-side (design §6: "every flag — mechanical AND judge-supplied — passes through assertContainedFlag"):
- No commissioned 4b test injects a fake judge that RETURNS a fabricated/union `spanId` (or bad `evidenceRef.spanId`) and asserts it is DROPPED. The AC-12 seam-map fixtures inject judges with valid ids (they test category mapping), and the AC-3 positive control's all-7 fake judge likewise. So no fixture ever feeds the judge-merge path a bad id.
- The AC-1 universal post-condition only validates OUTPUT flags; absent a bad-id-judge fixture it cannot distinguish "judge bad flag dropped" from "judge produced no bad flag" — it is zero-power against a judge-merge bypass.
- The only emit-bypass mutant named is the DETECTOR path; a judge-merge-appends-without-emit mutant has neither a commissioned test nor a listed mutant.

Design S-8 names "judge flag with a bad span id dropped" as a diff-check instrument, so the intent exists; the plan just fails to carry it into the 4b test table. This is the plan's own #2 risk and the component (an LLM judge in slice-2) most likely to fabricate a span.

Fix direction: commission a 4b test (AC-1 or AC-12): inject a fake judge returning one flag with a fabricated `spanId` and one with a valid `spanId`; assert the valid one survives, the fabricated one is absent, and the output stays well-shaped. Add a judge-merge-bypass entry to the step-9 mutant list so the emit chokepoint is proven on the judge path, not only the detector path.

### F3 (MINOR) — import-target inconsistency: §4 says public-surface AC tests "target the barrel", but the barrel is Step 5 while Step 4 claims the AC suite greens there
Location: §2 Step 4 green ("this is where most public-surface AC tests pass") vs §4 ("Public-surface AC tests target the barrel `lib/review`") vs §2 Step 5 (barrel `index.js` is new at step 5).

If the behavioral AC tests (AC-1..AC-15) import from `lib/review` (the barrel/`index.js`) per §4, they cannot be green at Step 4 because `index.js` does not exist until Step 5 — contradicting both "Step 4 is where most public-surface AC tests pass" and the "each step independently green" DAG claim. The consistent reading is that Step 4's `reviewDocuments.test.js` imports `./reviewDocuments.js` directly (deep import) and only AC-16/barrel surface waits for Step 5 — but then §4's "target the barrel" is wrong, and nothing exercises behavior through the actually-consumed surface (the barrel) until Step 5. Either resolution is behaviorally equivalent (the barrel re-exports the same function), so this is minor, but the plan should state which, so the TDD seat doesn't write barrel-importing AC tests that can't green at Step 4.

Fix direction: state that Step 4's behavioral AC tests import `./reviewDocuments.js` directly and that Step 5 adds a barrel-identity assertion (the barrel's `reviewDocuments` is the same function / same return shape), reconciling §4's "target the barrel" wording.
